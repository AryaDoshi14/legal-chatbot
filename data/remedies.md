# Procedural Remedies under Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 (BNSS)

### 1. Registration of First Information Report (FIR) in Cognizable Offences
- **Act & Section**: BNSS Section 173(1)
- **Title**: Information in cognizable cases
- **Trigger**: When any cognizable crime (such as theft, robbery, cheating, assault, rape, grievous hurt) has taken place.
- **Procedure**: Every information relating to the commission of a cognizable offence may be given orally or in writing to the officer in charge of a police station. If given orally, it must be reduced to writing, read over to the informant, and signed by the informant.

### 2. Zero FIR (Reporting Anywhere Irrespective of Territorial Jurisdiction)
- **Act & Section**: BNSS Section 173(1)
- **Title**: Information in cognizable cases
- **Trigger**: When an offence has occurred outside the territorial limits of the local police station where the victim is currently present or seeking immediate help.
- **Procedure**: Section 173(1) explicitly provides that information of a cognizable offence must be recorded "irrespective of the area where the offence is committed". The police station must register the Zero FIR and subsequently transfer the investigation to the jurisdictional police station.

### 3. Electronic FIR (e-FIR)
- **Act & Section**: BNSS Section 173(1)(ii)
- **Title**: Information in cognizable cases by electronic communication
- **Trigger**: When an informant chooses to report a cognizable offence digitally via email, portal, or police messaging app.
- **Procedure**: If information is given by electronic communication, it shall be taken on record by the police officer, provided it is signed by the informant within three days.

### 4. Mandatory Free Copy of FIR to the Informant or Victim
- **Act & Section**: BNSS Section 173(2)
- **Title**: Copy of FIR to informant or victim
- **Trigger**: Immediately upon registration of an FIR.
- **Procedure**: A copy of the recorded information (FIR) shall be given forthwith, free of cost, to the informant or the victim.

### 5. Preliminary Inquiry Before FIR in Moderate Offences
- **Act & Section**: BNSS Section 173(3)
- **Title**: Preliminary enquiry before registration of FIR
- **Trigger**: In offences punishable with imprisonment of 3 years or more but less than 7 years.
- **Procedure**: The officer in charge of a police station may, with prior permission of an officer not below the rank of Deputy Superintendent of Police, conduct a preliminary enquiry within fourteen days to ascertain whether a prima facie case exists before proceeding with investigation.

### 6. Remedy When Police Refuse to Register FIR (Complaint to SP / DCP)
- **Act & Section**: BNSS Section 173(4)
- **Title**: Refusal to record information and remedy by complaint to Superintendent of Police
- **Trigger**: When the officer in charge of a police station refuses to register an FIR for a cognizable offence.
- **Procedure**: Any person aggrieved by a refusal to record information may send the substance of such information in writing and by post to the Superintendent of Police. If satisfied that an offence has been disclosed, the SP shall either investigate the case himself or direct an investigation by a subordinate officer.

### 7. Reporting Non-Cognizable Offences (NCR / Police Diary)
- **Act & Section**: BNSS Section 174(1)
- **Title**: Information as to non-cognizable cases
- **Trigger**: When the reported offence is non-cognizable (e.g., simple non-grievous hurt, minor insult, non-violent dispute).
- **Procedure**: The officer in charge shall enter the substance of the information in the station diary (NCR) and refer the informant to the Judicial Magistrate. Police cannot arrest without a warrant in non-cognizable cases.

### 8. Bar on Investigation of Non-Cognizable Offences Without Magistrate Order
- **Act & Section**: BNSS Section 174(2)
- **Title**: Investigation of non-cognizable cases
- **Trigger**: Need for investigation in non-cognizable matters.
- **Procedure**: No police officer shall investigate a non-cognizable case without an order of a Magistrate having power to try such case or commit the case for trial.

### 9. Magistrate Ordering Investigation into Cognizable Offence
- **Act & Section**: BNSS Section 175(3)
- **Title**: Magistrate's power to order investigation
- **Trigger**: When police fail or refuse to investigate, or after an application to the Superintendent of Police has failed.
- **Procedure**: Any Magistrate empowered under section 210 may order a police investigation into a cognizable case. The applicant must have first approached the police officer under 173(1) and the Superintendent under 173(4) supported by an affidavit.

### 10. Notice of Appearance Instead of Immediate Arrest (Offences under 7 Years)
- **Act & Section**: BNSS Section 35(3)
- **Title**: Notice of appearance before police officer
- **Trigger**: Cognizable offences where maximum imprisonment is up to seven years and arrest is not deemed necessary.
- **Procedure**: The police officer shall issue a notice of appearance directing the person against whom a reasonable complaint has been made to appear before him. If the person complies and continues to comply, they shall not be arrested unless reasons are recorded in writing.

### 11. Prior Sanction Before Arrest of Elderly or Infirm Persons
- **Act & Section**: BNSS Section 35(7)
- **Title**: Arrest of elderly or infirm persons
- **Trigger**: Arrest of any person who is infirm or above the age of sixty years for offences punishable with less than three years.
- **Procedure**: No arrest shall be made without prior permission of an officer not below the rank of Deputy Superintendent of Police.

### 12. Special Safeguards for Arrest of Women
- **Act & Section**: BNSS Section 43(1) Proviso
- **Title**: Arrest of women and night-time protection
- **Trigger**: Arrest of a female accused.
- **Procedure**: A female shall be arrested only by a woman police officer. Except in exceptional circumstances, no woman shall be arrested after sunset and before sunrise, and where exceptional circumstances exist, prior permission of a Judicial Magistrate First Class must be obtained.

### 13. Right of Arrested Person to Inform Family or Friend
- **Act & Section**: BNSS Section 48
- **Title**: Obligation of person making arrest to inform relative or friend
- **Trigger**: Immediately upon arrest of any person.
- **Procedure**: The police officer making the arrest must inform any of the arrested person's friends, relatives, or nominated person about the arrest and the location where the arrested person is being held.

### 14. Right to Medical Examination of Arrested Person
- **Act & Section**: BNSS Section 53
- **Title**: Examination of arrested person by medical officer
- **Trigger**: When any person is arrested.
- **Procedure**: The arrested person shall be examined by a medical officer in the service of the Central or State Government immediately after arrest. A copy of the medical examination report must be furnished to the arrested person or their nominated representative.

### 15. Medical Examination of Victim of Sexual Offences
- **Act & Section**: BNSS Section 184
- **Title**: Medical examination of the victim of rape
- **Trigger**: Where an offence of rape or attempt to commit rape is under investigation.
- **Procedure**: The victim shall be sent to a registered medical practitioner in a Government hospital or local authority hospital within twenty-four hours from the time information is received. The examination requires consent of the victim or her guardian.

### 16. Right to Free Immediate Medical Treatment for Victims
- **Act & Section**: BNSS Section 397
- **Title**: Treatment of victims
- **Trigger**: Any injury or trauma suffered by a victim of crime.
- **Procedure**: All hospitals, public or private, whether run by the Central Government, State Government, local bodies, or other persons, shall immediately provide first-aid or medical treatment free of cost to victims of offences, and inform the police of the incident.

### 17. Production Before Magistrate Within 24 Hours
- **Act & Section**: BNSS Section 58 and Section 187(1)
- **Title**: Detention beyond twenty-four hours prohibited
- **Trigger**: Arrest and detention of any person.
- **Procedure**: No police officer shall detain an arrested person in custody for a longer period than twenty-four hours (exclusive of the time necessary for the journey from the place of arrest to the Magistrate's Court) without an order of a Magistrate.

### 18. Remand to Police or Judicial Custody
- **Act & Section**: BNSS Section 187(2)
- **Title**: Remand during investigation
- **Trigger**: When investigation cannot be completed within 24 hours.
- **Procedure**: The Magistrate may authorize detention of the accused person in custody for a term not exceeding fifteen days in the whole or in parts at any time during the initial 40 or 60 days of the investigation period.

### 19. Statutory Default Bail on Incomplete Investigation
- **Act & Section**: BNSS Section 187(3)
- **Title**: Default bail upon expiry of 60 or 90 days
- **Trigger**: When police fail to file a chargesheet within the statutory period.
- **Procedure**: The accused person is entitled to default bail if the investigation is not completed within 90 days (for offences punishable with death, life imprisonment, or imprisonment of 10 years or more) or within 60 days (for all other offences), provided the accused is prepared to furnish bail.

### 20. Filing a Private Criminal Complaint Before Judicial Magistrate
- **Act & Section**: BNSS Section 223
- **Title**: Examination of complainant
- **Trigger**: When police refuse to act or when an aggrieved citizen directly petitions the judiciary.
- **Procedure**: A Magistrate taking cognizance of an offence on complaint shall examine the complainant and the witnesses present upon oath. In cases against an accused who is not a public servant, the Magistrate must give the accused an opportunity of being heard before taking cognizance.

### 21. Inquiry and Postponement of Process by Magistrate
- **Act & Section**: BNSS Section 225
- **Title**: Postponement of issue of process
- **Trigger**: When Magistrate needs to verify if prima facie grounds exist for issuing summons/warrant.
- **Procedure**: The Magistrate may postpone the issue of process against the accused and either inquire into the case himself or direct an investigation by a police officer to decide whether there is sufficient ground for proceeding.

### 22. Dismissal of Groundless Complaint
- **Act & Section**: BNSS Section 226
- **Title**: Dismissal of complaint
- **Trigger**: If after considering complainant's statement and inquiry, no prima facie case exists.
- **Procedure**: If the Magistrate is of the opinion that there is no sufficient ground for proceeding, he shall dismiss the complaint, briefly recording his reasons for doing so.

### 23. Bail as a Matter of Right in Bailable Offences
- **Act & Section**: BNSS Section 478
- **Title**: In what cases bail to be taken (Bailable offences)
- **Trigger**: When a person accused of a bailable offence is arrested or detained without warrant.
- **Procedure**: Such person shall be released on bail as a matter of right if they are prepared at any time while in police custody or before Court to give bail. If indigent and unable to furnish surety, the Court may discharge them on executing a personal bond without sureties.

### 24. Regular Bail in Non-Bailable Offences
- **Act & Section**: BNSS Section 480
- **Title**: When bail may be taken in case of non-bailable offence
- **Trigger**: When a person accused of a non-bailable offence is arrested or appears before Court.
- **Procedure**: The Court has discretion to release the person on bail, but cannot grant bail if there appear reasonable grounds for believing they are guilty of an offence punishable with death or imprisonment for life, subject to exceptions for minors under 16, women, and sick or infirm persons.

### 25. Anticipatory Bail (Pre-Arrest Bail)
- **Act & Section**: BNSS Section 482
- **Title**: Direction for grant of bail to person apprehending arrest
- **Trigger**: When any person has reason to believe that they may be arrested on an accusation of having committed a non-bailable offence.
- **Procedure**: The person may apply to the High Court or the Court of Session for a direction that in the event of arrest, they shall be released on bail, subject to conditions such as joining investigation and not tampering with evidence.

### 26. Special Bail Powers and Cancellation of Bail
- **Act & Section**: BNSS Section 483
- **Title**: Special powers of High Court or Court of Session regarding bail
- **Trigger**: Review of bail orders or necessity to cancel bail.
- **Procedure**: The High Court or Court of Session may direct that any person who has been released on bail be arrested and committed to custody if bail conditions are violated or justice demands.

### 27. Free Legal Aid to Accused at State Expense
- **Act & Section**: BNSS Section 341
- **Title**: Legal aid to accused at State expense
- **Trigger**: Where in a trial before the Court of Session, the accused is not represented by an advocate and lacks sufficient means.
- **Procedure**: The Court shall assign an advocate for his defense at the expense of the State.

### 28. Victim Compensation Scheme
- **Act & Section**: BNSS Section 396
- **Title**: Victim compensation scheme
- **Trigger**: Where a victim suffers loss, injury, or death due to a crime and needs rehabilitation.
- **Procedure**: Every State Government in coordination with the Central Government prepares a scheme for providing funds for compensation to the victim or their dependents. The Court or District Legal Services Authority (DLSA) can award compensation.

### 29. Witness Protection Scheme
- **Act & Section**: BNSS Section 398
- **Title**: Witness protection scheme
- **Trigger**: When a witness in a criminal proceeding faces threat, intimidation, or harassment.
- **Procedure**: Every State Government shall prepare and notify a Witness Protection Scheme to ensure protection of witnesses during investigation and trial.

### 30. Quashing of Frivolous FIR / Inherent Powers of High Court
- **Act & Section**: BNSS Section 528
- **Title**: Saving of inherent powers of High Court
- **Trigger**: Abuse of the criminal process, false or malicious prosecution, or non-cognizable civil disputes given criminal color.
- **Procedure**: The High Court has inherent powers to make such orders as may be necessary to give effect to any order under this Sanhita, or to prevent abuse of the process of any Court, or otherwise to secure the ends of justice (equivalent to old Section 482 CrPC).
